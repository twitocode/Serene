
Executive Summary
A review of recent sources and student feedback reveals multiple pressing student‑centred issues at McMaster University. These include mental health (long counselling waitlists, limited specialist care), housing (high rents, poor conditions, scarce on‑campus space), financial aid (rising debt, OSAP changes, emergency needs), academic advising/registration (overloaded advisors, full courses, no waitlists), accessibility (physical and service gaps), campus safety (notably sexual violence and crime incidents), transit/parking (limited parking, costly permits), food security (student food insecurity, stigma), career services (lack of targeted support), student governance (low engagement, transparency issues) and diversity/inclusion (equity gaps, free-speech conflicts) as well as COVID‑19 protocols (class suspensions, vaccine mandates). Affected groups span all students but are acute for undergraduates, international and Indigenous students, and other marginalized communities. McMaster has responded via initiatives (e.g. new residence construction
, emergency bursaries and food banks
, mental-health funding) but gaps remain (e.g. waitlists, limited awareness of resources, coordination, and support).

We propose a comprehensive student‑focused website with features addressing each issue. For example, an integrated "Issue Navigator" could let students select their group and concern (e.g. housing, mental health) and receive tailored resources, hotline/chat links and service maps. Other features include real-time queue trackers for advising or counselling appointments, interactive housing/job boards, FAQ wizards for OSAP/registration queries, and anonymous feedback channels for governance/diversity concerns. Each feature is evaluated for impact (reach and benefit), feasibility (technical and administrative) and cost (high/med/low). A comparison table maps issues to features and priorities. Success will be measured by KPIs like usage metrics, wait-time reductions, user satisfaction, and equity of access. An implementation roadmap (phased by planning, design, development, testing, launch) details tasks, timeline and stakeholders (students, IT, wellness staff, faculty and union representatives).

Overall, a well‑designed website can fill communication gaps, streamline access to existing supports, and provide data-driven feedback loops to improve student wellbeing and equity. (Details follow.)

Issues & Evidence
Mental Health Services
Students report long wait times and limited support at the Student Wellness Centre (SWC). One student writes: “impossible to get a psychiatrist…Many people are waiting since November…ends up ~4 weeks to see someone”
. A McMaster health forum report notes that high demand and limited resources mean “students with lower‑priority needs may have to wait for weeks for subsequent appointments.” The SWC is described as a “primary care” service (front‑line support) not equipped for frequent psychiatric care
. Minoritized groups (e.g. international students without OHIP) are particularly affected by lack of accessible mental health care
.

Affected groups: All students, especially undergrads, international and Indigenous students facing cultural/language barriers
.

Current responses & gaps: McMaster funds the SWC through student fees and partner programs (Good2Talk, peer support
) and the MSU runs Wellness initiatives. However, the SWC has budget/space constraints and excludes some cohorts (e.g. MBA/divinity students)
. Campus resources exist (counselling, PeerSupport groups, emergency mental-health helplines) but are hard to navigate, leading to fragmented care
. Communication gaps (faculty not informed, stigma) remain
.

Website Solutions: A dedicated Mental Health Hub with self‑screening tools, clear guidance on booking counselling (including wait estimates), virtual peer‑support forums, and symptom-monitoring reminders. Integrate online chatbots and links to 24/7 helplines. Important UX: empathetic tone, accessible design (e.g. screen-reader friendly) and confidentiality assurances. Content could include short videos on coping strategies, FAQs on SWC process, and multilingual resources. Integrations/APIs: potential links to booking systems or anonymous surveys (data privacy critical).

Priority: High impact (supports large student population), medium cost (content creation + simple chatbot).

Housing & Off-Campus Living
Affordable, safe housing is a major concern. A recent MSU survey shows housing is a top student priority and a determinant of whether students stay in Hamilton
. Students report “helplessness” with off-campus rentals: “many students…struggle to find decent housing for <$1000… Safe housing is a human right.”
. Respondents favor more regulations on landlords even if rents rise
. Only 16% live on campus; many rent elsewhere in Hamilton but face rising rents ($800–$1000+ monthly).

Affected groups: Primarily undergraduates and graduate renters, commuters (including first-years not in residence). Mature or part-time students who live with families may be less affected by housing, but affordable housing benefits retention of Indigenous and low‑income students.

Current responses & gaps: McMaster is expanding on‑campus housing aggressively. Lincoln Alexander Hall (1366 beds, opening 2026) will allow guaranteed first-year housing
. Over 1,100 beds have been added since 2019
. However, these solve only first-year demand; off‑campus students still face high rent, renovictions, poor maintenance. The university lacks a unified off-campus housing support (though the Hamilton survey indicates strong student advocacy
).

Website Solutions: An Off-Campus Housing Portal. Features: interactive housing listings/map (with filters for price, pets, etc.), a landlord rating system, and clear guides on tenant rights and McMaster’s renoviction bylaw. UX should be mobile-friendly and searchable. Include forums for sublets and roommate matching. Integrate Google Maps API for location search; possibly link to municipal data (e.g. building inspections). Privacy: anonymize user posts. Also highlight on-campus applications timeline.

Priority: High impact (most commuters/renters affected), medium feasibility (need to aggregate data, moderate development).

Financial Aid & Affordability
Many students face financial stress. For example, new OSAP rules have increased loans: MSU President notes “students are already struggling to afford food, books, textbooks”
. In 2026, Ontario shifted from 85% OSAP grants to 25%, raising debt fears
. Simultaneously, tuition is rising (tuition freeze lifted, allowing 2% increases)
. Emergencies (eviction, job loss) further strain budgets.

Affected groups: All students, with high impact on low‑income, undergrads, and those in professional programs with no OHIP (pharmacy, etc). International students pay higher tuition and may lack OSAP, heightening vulnerability.

Current responses & gaps: McMaster offers bursaries and an “Emergency Financial Support” program for crises
. There’s the MSU Food Collective Centre (free monthly groceries)
. However, many are unaware of or embarrassed to access these. The system is reactive (aid after need) rather than preventative. Lack of centralized info on aid deadlines and eligibility is a gap.

Website Solutions: A Financial Aid Navigator. Features: personalized OSAP/payment calculator, scholarship/bursary finder (with dynamic filtering), and an emergency aid application tracker. Include budgeting tools and links to campus resources (Food Bank, Mac’s Money Centre
). UX: interactive sliders to show loan vs grant breakdown, and clear workflows to apply for funds. Data integration: OSAP API (if available) to fetch eligibility info; encryption for any personal data. Privacy: no sensitive info stored without consent.

Priority: High impact (reducing student financial stress), medium feasibility (requires careful info architecture but builds on existing data).

Academic Advising & Registration
Students report difficulty getting academic guidance. One noted “wait times are horrible…it’s impossible to get a hold of academic advising. The longer I wait, the more classes get filled up.”
. Course enrolment is compounded by limited seats and complicated priority rules (e.g. upper-year registration windows). There is no automated waitlist for many courses, leaving students confused about next steps
.

Affected groups: Primarily undergraduates registering for core courses (Science, Engineering, etc). Possibly first-years who lack guidance, and transfer students. Graduate students have separate advising, but similar issues (course selection, supervisor availability).

Current responses & gaps: Each faculty has academic advisors (contacts listed on Registrar’s site
) and University offers self‑serve tools (Advisement Report in MOSAIC
). However, students find advisors overbooked, and confusion arises over enrolment rules. There is no single “one-stop” source for advising: information is scattered among faculty sites.

Website Solutions: An Academic Support Center. Features: an Ask Advisor system (AI chatbot trained on FAQs about degree requirements), a scheduling portal or real-time queue indicator for advising (showing virtual/in-person queue length), and an interactive degree planner visualizer (mock-up of Advisement Report). Also a “Registration Help” guide explaining enrolment appointments and priority rules (e.g. class-year order, lab restrictions). Integrations: connect to Mosaic APIs for seat availability and waitlists (if open), and Zoom/chat for drop‑in queues. Privacy: ensure queries to advisors remain confidential.

Priority: Medium impact (affects academic success), medium cost (requires content & possibly AI integration).

Accessibility & Inclusion
Physical and academic accessibility remain issues. Students with disabilities report barriers: e.g. lecture hall seating blocked by stairs, few elevator-accessible classrooms, and limited building accommodations
 (though that’s from 2014, conditions likely persist in older buildings). There are accessibility policies and a SAS office, but navigation and awareness are problems.

Affected groups: Students with disabilities (mobility, hearing, visual, learning) and those requiring accommodations. Also intersectional (international students with limited English, etc.).

Current responses & gaps: McMaster has an Accessibility Hub and Student Accessibility Services (SAS)
. But gaps include inconsistent awareness of accessible entrances, delayed accommodation processing, and siloed info.

Website Solutions: An Accessibility Resources section. Features: campus maps with accessible routes (wheelchair paths, elevators), a consolidated FAQ for SAS accommodations (how to register, timelines), and an anonymous feedback form for accessibility issues. UX: high-contrast design, screen-reader compatibility, adjustable text sizes. Possibly integrate with Google Maps API to flag accessible parking. Ensure privacy for personal requests.

Priority: Medium impact (ensures equity), low cost (mainly info aggregation).

Campus Safety & Sexual Violence
Campus safety incidents and climate of security are a concern. High-profile cases (e.g. graduate student suspensions, professor misconduct) have drawn attention to sexual assault and harassment
. In 2020, McMaster suspended two faculty and banned a graduate student amid a sexual violence investigation
. Violent crime (e.g. off-campus assaults) also impacts sense of safety.

Affected groups: All students, with particular urgency for women, 2SLGBTQ+ students, international students (who may feel isolated), and minority groups. Survivors of assault.

Current responses & gaps: McMaster has a Sexual Violence Policy Office, mandatory training (poster in safety report
), and police liaison. However, students still report fear of reporting, and limited awareness of safety resources (red phones, escorts).

Website Solutions: A Safety & Reporting Hub. Features: 24/7 alert buttons (link to Campus Security chat/911), interactive map of safe shelters/call boxes, and clear guidance on reporting harassment/assault (how-to, timelines). Integrate anonymous tip-submission forms. UX: prominent placement of “In Crisis? Get Help” banner, multilingual options. Data: possibly connect to real-time alerts (e.g. campus status RSS). Privacy: ensure anonymity options (anonymous tip).

Priority: High impact (critical well-being), medium feasibility (leverages known safety data).

Transit & Parking
Commuting and parking are pain points. Campus parking is limited/expensive: one grad student reports paying “~$60/week on parking” yet still needing to shuttle to classes
. Only select lots are available to students (many on waitlists
). Public transit (HSR) is available, but students often find schedules inconvenient, and disruptions (strikes) have affected many.

Affected groups: On-campus commuters (particularly mature or faculty-staff students), and international students without cars. Undergrads usually live on res or take transit.

Current responses & gaps: McMaster Parking Services publishes permit info, and there are shuttle buses. However, student grievances (high cost, remote lots) persist
. There’s no unified transit-guide for students (HSR passes, GO, carpool options).

Website Solutions: A Transit & Parking Center. Features: real-time lot availability (or permit waitlist status), shuttle bus tracking (via transit APIs), and an HSR/GO trip planner tool for Hamilton/Toronto commutes. Also information on special programs (carpool sign-up, bike routes). UX: intuitive search for routes; map UI. Integrations: city transit and parking APIs, Google Maps. Privacy: minimal (no personal data, just directions).

Priority: Medium impact (many students affected), medium effort (coordination with transit agencies).

Food Security
Food insecurity is significant. Surveys find ~25% of McMaster students view “challenges in obtaining food” as normal student life
. Many live on tight budgets (tuition/rent compete with food
). Marginalized groups suffer most: e.g. 56% of Indigenous and 75% of Black students report food insecurity
.

Affected groups: Low-income students (including Indigenous, Black, 2SLGBTQ+, disabled, international who lack family support).

Current responses & gaps: The MSU Food Collective Centre (in the Hub) offers monthly groceries and free meals
. There are emergency meal cards from financial aid. However, stigma and lack of awareness deter usage
. Data on usage is sparse.

Website Solutions: A Food Security Portal. Features: schedules of food services (Food Collective, “Lockers of Love”), an interactive map of on-campus free meal locations/times, and “food guide” (easy cheap recipes, campus grocery discounts). Also a confidential survey tool to report unmet food needs (to gather data for advocacy). UX: stigma-free, inclusive imagery. Integrations: link to MSU food bank system if digital sign-ups, and to community kitchens. Privacy: optional anonymity for surveys.

Priority: High impact for equity, low cost (info aggregation & light interactivity).

Career Services
Career support can be uneven. Some students feel career advising is generic, or unaware of programs (co‑op vs networking). International and first-gen students may lack guidance. Diversity barriers exist: a “Career Access Program” was launched for marginalized students.

Affected groups: All students, especially those without family networks (first-gen, immigrants), and equity‑seeking groups facing employment bias.

Current responses & gaps: The Student Success Centre offers advising, workshops and a new equity‑focused program (Career Access Program)
. Nonetheless, students still want more accessible industry connections and clarity on careers outside their major.

Website Solutions: A Career Hub. Features: an AI‑driven career quiz matching interests with McMaster programs and resources, an event calendar of career fairs/volunteer opps, and a spotlight on DEI resources (mentorship programs for underrepresented students). UX: personalized dashboards (save fav jobs, mentors). Integration: handshake or job board API; calendar feeds. Privacy: user accounts for saved items.

Priority: Medium impact, medium effort (requires coordination with career services).

Student Governance & Engagement
Low voter turnout and transparency issues plague student government. Controversies (e.g. election irregularities) have arisen informally
. Many students feel disconnected from decision‑making.

Affected groups: All students (as constituents), especially those in distant programs (graduate, off-campus) who feel under-represented.

Current responses & gaps: MSU holds elections and referenda, but public information is scattered. Grad student government (GSA) is separate. There’s no unified platform for student voices.

Website Solutions: A Student Voice Center. Features: archives of MSU/GSA meeting minutes, easy polls on key issues, and links to run for office or petition. Possibly a “Town Hall” livestream schedule. UX: civic engagement style, with notifications for voting periods. Integration: event livestream (YouTube) and e‑voting integration (if any). Privacy: protect voter anonymity in polls.

Priority: Low‑medium (important for culture, but less immediate well-being), low cost (mostly content linking).

Diversity, Equity & Inclusion
Students report issues around microaggressions, racialized experiences, and debates over free speech. (For example, the Jordan Peterson lecture protest in 2017 highlighted tensions
.) Some feel equity offices need more visibility.

Affected groups: Racialized, Indigenous, 2SLGBTQ+ and other equity‑seeking students primarily.

Current responses & gaps: McMaster has an Office of Equity & Inclusion, cultural centers (e.g. Indigenous Education). However, students often lack a single source to navigate these resources or report incidents of discrimination anonymously.

Website Solutions: An Equity Resources Portal. Features: directories of support units (counselors, cultural clubs), an anonymous incident reporting form, and a “Know Your Rights” toolkit. Also highlight diversity events. UX: inclusive language, privacy-first design. Integrations: none sensitive. Privacy: high – allow anonymous submissions, SSL encryption.

Priority: High impact (addresses systemic issues), medium cost (requires vetting sensitive content).

COVID‑19 & Health Protocols
The pandemic caused major disruptions. McMaster suspended in‑person classes in March 2020
 and later implemented vaccine mandates. Four students legally challenged the vaccine mandate but the court upheld McMaster’s approach
. Though mandates have lifted, lingering issues include building ventilation, hybrid learning concerns, and health communications.

Affected groups: All campus communities, especially immunocompromised students, and international students facing travel restrictions.

Current responses & gaps: McMaster followed PHO guidelines, offered vaccinations, and provided COVID info pages
. Gaps included confusion over changing policies and limited mental-health checks during isolation.

Website Solutions: A Health & Safety Portal. Features: an archive of COVID protocols (mask, vaccine info), links to campus testing and immunization clinic, and a “Health FAQs” section (flu, COVID updates). Also a symptom checker chatbot for general health. UX: simple calm tone. Data: API integration with health advisories. Privacy: no personal data, optional anonymous questions.

Priority: Medium (lower demand post‑pandemic), low cost (mostly info).

Success Metrics & KPIs
Key metrics should be tracked post-launch. Examples include: Usage statistics (page views, unique users of each hub); Appointment wait times before/after online queue implementation; Survey feedback on whether students feel better informed; Resource engagement (downloads of guides, chatbot interactions); Equity measures (increased access by marginalized students, lower food-bank usage stigma); Policy outcomes (e.g. if enough feedback drives housing policy changes). For example, an increase in online bursary applications or hotline calls might indicate better reach. Collect data via web analytics, follow-up surveys, and partnering with campus offices for outcomes (e.g. counselling center reports shorter wait times).

Stakeholders: Students (via surveys and student unions/MSU), McMaster IT Services, Registrar/Advising, Student Wellness, Student Financial Aid, Facilities (housing), Student Accessibility Services, Campus Safety, HSR/parking teams, and campus news/media for promotion. Early involvement of student groups (e.g. Indigenous Student Affairs, international office) is crucial to ensure inclusivity and uptake.

Throughout, evaluate in each phase. A pilot of core features (e.g. financial aid tools, housing board) could launch by Fall 2026, with iterative expansion (adding forums, chatbots) by Spring 2027.

Success measures: Short-term – system usage and positive user feedback; Medium-term – reduced service waitlists, increased uptake of supports (e.g. more bursary applications, food bank visits by higher-income threshold students as awareness); Long-term – improved student satisfaction scores (e.g. NSSE or internal surveys), retention of students (especially from underrepresented groups)
, and better health/academic outcomes.

By closing information gaps, centralizing resources, and leveraging technology for triage and triaging (e.g. chatbots to answer FAQs), the proposed website can significantly enhance student wellbeing and agency.

Sources: Authoritative McMaster and local news, surveys and student union reports (MSU “Your City Survey 2025”), student forum accounts, and research briefs
. Each solution aligns with evidence of need and leverages existing campus programs to improve access and equity.
